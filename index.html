Abstract

Language-Queried Audio Source Separation (LASS) enables open-vocabulary sound separation by conditioning on natural language descriptions. While existing LASS methods rely on task-specific training, we explore whether pretrained diffusion models, originally designed for audio generation, can inherently perform separation without additional training. In this study, we introduce a training-free framework that leverages generative priors for zero-shot LASS. We examine naïve adaptations of diffusion models to LASS and identify key limitations arising from modality-specific challenges. To address these issues, we propose Diffusion-Guided Mask Optimization (DGMO), a test-time optimization framework that refines spectrogram masks for precise, input-aligned separation. Our approach demonstrates that pretrained diffusion models can be effectively repurposed for source separation, achieving competitive performance without task-specific supervision. This work expands the application of diffusion models beyond generation, establishing a new paradigm for zero-shot audio separation.

Diffusion-Guided Mask Optimization

We introduce a novel training-free LASS framework based on diffusion models, integrating mask optimization and generative refinement into a unified process composed of reference generation and mask optimization stages.
